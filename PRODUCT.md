# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Two buyer contexts, one skill set. Startup founders and CTOs who are adding AI or
LLM features and need the architecture right — and secure — from the first
commit, with no in-house AI-security expertise. And established teams handling
sensitive data (compliance, legal, regulated operations) who need the higher bar:
data that stays under their control, an air-gapped option, and findings they can
put in front of an auditor or a board. Both arrive because they are wiring
models, agents, or connectors like MCP into real systems and do not want to find
out later what could go wrong. Whoever ends up using a resulting tool is often
non-technical; the person booking the call is technical enough to judge the work.

## Product Purpose

A landing page whose single job is to turn a qualified visitor into a booked
call. It sells one thing: a specialist who architects and builds secure AI
systems — RAG, agents, access control, document intelligence — that run in
production, online or fully air-gapped. The four products (NIS2 Analyzer, Lex
Legal, DocSense, TankSlim) are the portfolio: proof the specialist ships real
systems, not decks. Success is a steady flow of calls from the two target buyer
contexts, pre-qualified by the security framing so wrong-fit leads self-select
out.

## Positioning

Secure AI systems, architected and built by one specialist — production-grade,
explainable to the people who sign off, and yours to run online or air-gapped.
The claim every section reinforces: security is designed in from the first line,
not patched on after a leak.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: Book a call. Secondary, for visitors not ready to book: See the
  work — scroll to the four products.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: secure AI systems,
  production-grade, online or air-gapped, built by one specialist.
- Belief ladder: first, this person actually ships production AI systems, not
  prototypes or slideware; then, security here is structural, decided at
  architecture time rather than bolted on later; then, my sensitive data and work
  stay under my control — hosted with hard boundaries, or fully air-gapped on my
  own infrastructure; then, the output is explainable in plain language to the
  non-technical people who have to approve it; finally, a short call is low-risk —
  no pitch deck, no obligation.
- Proof on hand: four shipped products, shown in the Solutions section — NIS2
  Analyzer (compliance-audit SaaS, in production), Lex Legal (file-level access
  control for law firms, in production), DocSense (document Q&A with sourced
  answers, in production), TankSlim (coming soon). No client names, testimonials,
  or logos yet.

## Brand Personality

Quiet, precise, trustworthy. The voice of an understated expert who does not
oversell — calm, specific, plain-spoken. Security is treated as a given, not a
selling point to shout about. No hype, no urgency, no superlatives; confidence is
carried by specificity and by the work on show. It should read like a direct
conversation with the person who does the building — one operator, no account
layer, no telephone game.

## Anti-references

Not a generic AI-SaaS marketing page: no gradient-blob hero, no "supercharge /
unlock / 10x your workflow" copy, no identical three-up emoji feature grid, no
purple-on-white. Not a generic freelancer portfolio: no skill bars, no "Hi, I'm
___ and I make cool things", no template chrome, no everything-for-everyone
breadth. Not big-consultancy enterprise: no stock handshake or office
photography, no navy-and-grey palette, no dense buzzword paragraphs, no "digital
transformation" register. Not AI hype: no neon glitch effects, no
"revolutionary", no countdown-timer urgency, no aggressive superlatives.

## Design Principles

Proof over promises. Every capability claim points at a product the visitor can
look at; adjectives do not do work that evidence should.

Security is the position, not a feature. The page leads with secure-by-design,
online or air-gapped, and keeps returning to it — it is the reason to pick a
specialist over a generalist.

Speak to the signer, not just the engineer. Copy stays in language a
non-technical stakeholder can follow; that translation ability is part of what is
being sold.

Quiet confidence. Understatement sells expertise here — one calm CTA, no urgency,
no hard sell, no superlatives.

One operator, direct. The value is no handoffs and no account layer; the page
should read like talking to the person who does the work.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA. Body text meets 4.5:1 contrast against its background (the
muted grey on near-black was lifted for this reason); large text meets 3:1. Every
animation — scroll-reveal headings, aurora fields, SVG motifs — has a
prefers-reduced-motion path that renders the final state with no motion, backed
by a global kill switch in globals.css. Status is never signalled by colour alone
(the Available now / Coming soon pills pair a dot with a text label). Keyboard
focus order follows visual order, and interactive elements are real links and
buttons.
